\subsection{RQ4: Which prompt components actually carry the performance?}\label{subsec:rq4}

We ablate the high-level planner's system prompt one component at a time and measure the
resulting loading-mission performance. All conditions share the same frozen low-level controller,
the same $n{=}100$ held-out seeds, the same Qwen3.5-27B planner, and the same inputs
(RGB cabin view $+$ Cartesian top-down height map); only the prompt text differs.
The full prompt has eight components: (1) image interpretation, (2) kinematic reach envelope,
(3) vertical-passability test, (4) clearance rules and a three-step path-safety check,
(5) task template, (6) planning order, (7) output schema, (8) two few-shot examples.

\paragraph{Prompt scaffolding is nearly free to remove.}
Table~\ref{tab:rq4-id} shows that on in-distribution scenes every component can be deleted
without a significant loss: cutting the prompt from 263 lines to a 48-line \emph{minimal}
variant (coordinate frame $+$ task $+$ output schema only) costs $0.02$ success ($p{=}0.41$).
Only the few-shot examples approach significance ($-0.06$, $p{=}0.052$).

\begin{table}[t]\centering
\caption{Prompt-component ablation, in-distribution scenes ($n{=}100$).
$p$: two-proportion $z$-test against \emph{full}.}\label{tab:rq4-id}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
\toprule
prompt & success & safe-succ. & collision & $p$ \\
\midrule
full (263 lines)      & 0.98 & 0.72 & 0.28 & --- \\
$-$ kinematics        & 0.97 & 0.71 & 0.27 & 0.651 \\
$-$ clearance         & 0.97 & 0.71 & 0.27 & 0.651 \\
$-$ CoT procedure     & 0.97 & 0.68 & 0.31 & 0.651 \\
$-$ examples          & 0.92 & 0.66 & 0.28 & 0.052 \\
minimal (48 lines)    & 0.96 & 0.71 & 0.27 & 0.407 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Under perceptual ambiguity, only the examples matter.}
In the \emph{confusable} setting (pile and truck rendered as boxes of the same size as the
obstacles, so geometry alone cannot assign object roles) overall success drops to $0.63$, yet
removing the kinematics or the safety component still changes nothing; removing the examples
costs $0.18$ success (Table~\ref{tab:rq4-conf}). The failure is a planning failure, not an
execution one: the minimum distance from any planned sub-goal to the true pile grows from
$0.54$\,m to $1.48$\,m and the fraction of plans that target the pile within $0.5$\,m falls from
$0.66$ to $0.28$, while the number of blobs labelled \texttt{obstacle} rises from $2.04$ to
$2.82$ (ground truth $2.68$) --- without a demonstration the planner labels the box-shaped
pile and truck as obstacles to avoid.

\paragraph{Why the null results are a property of the benchmark, not of the components.}
Two confounds make Tables~\ref{tab:rq4-id}--\ref{tab:rq4-conf} unable to falsify the kinematics
and safety components. First, the ablations \emph{leak}: deleting a section leaves the same
knowledge in the sections that are never deleted (Table~\ref{tab:rq4-leak}). Consistently, the
plans are unchanged when \S4 is removed --- mean tip-path clearance $+0.35$\,m in both conditions,
obstacle-free-path rate $0.85$ vs.\ $0.88$, maximum sub-goal height $1.94$ vs.\ $1.99$\,m.
Second, the scenes never \emph{bind} the constraints: no target ever leaves the reach envelope and
no obstacle is ever too tall to fly over (Table~\ref{tab:rq4-bind}, ID column). A component whose
knowledge is never needed cannot be shown to matter.
A third, metric-level confound affects collisions specifically: the planner emits bucket-tip
way-points, but a collision is recorded whenever \emph{any} arm link sweeps an obstacle. Plans
whose tip path is geometrically clear still collide $0.46$ of the time, and the way-point-only
baseline collides \emph{less} than the fully-scaffolded planner ($0.36$ vs.\ $0.50$), so tip-space
clearance rules cannot move this metric.

\paragraph{With a leak-free ablation on binding scenes, the safety component does carry weight.}
We re-run the safety ablation on \textsc{tall} scenes and remove the safety knowledge from
\emph{every} location in the prompt (passability test, clearance rules, path check, the
``lift above $h_{\max}{+}0.5$'' clause of the task template, the \texttt{passable} and
\texttt{clearance\_check} output fields, and the detour demonstration in the few-shot examples),
keeping perception, the coordinate frame and the reach table intact. Because execution collisions
saturate on \textsc{tall} scenes for reasons outside the planner's control, the primary metric is
plan-level: \emph{plan-safe} is the fraction of plans whose entire HOME$\to$sub-goal poly-line
stays outside every obstacle box.

\paragraph{Takeaway.}
The marginal value of prompt engineering is a function of task difficulty, not of prompt length:
once perception is solved, a $48$-line prompt matches a $263$-line one. Apparent null results for
individual components must be read together with (i) whether the ablation is leak-free and
(ii) whether the scene distribution makes the removed knowledge necessary --- otherwise an
ablation measures the benchmark rather than the prompt.
